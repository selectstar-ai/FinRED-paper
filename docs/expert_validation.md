# Expert Validation: Per-Question FGI Results

This document provides the detailed per-question Focus Group Interview (FGI) results
referenced in the paper. Following social-science FGI methodology, we conducted one
focus group interview and two discussion sessions with **12 financial security experts**
from the Financial Security Institute (FSI) to assess the proposed two-level financial
risk taxonomy. Experts provided five-point Likert responses with qualitative feedback,
and we computed Cohen's κ and Krippendorff's α for agreement beyond chance.

## Per-Question Detail

### (1) Representational Adequacy (Q1)
Ten of twelve experts (83.3%) agreed that the taxonomy adequately captures the essential
threat types relevant to financial institutions and consumers, yielding a mean score of
4.46 (SD = 0.49). The experts emphasized that the taxonomy aligns well with the technical
and regulatory risk categories, confirming its representational breadth across financial
domains. Several experts suggested clarifying conceptual overlaps between *R2. Financial
Crime* and *R4. Consumer Rights Violation* to improve categorical distinction and
stakeholder mapping. Inter-rater reliability for this dimension was substantial
(κ = 0.78, α = 0.80).

### (2) Inter-Expert Consistency (Q2)
The experts showed strong consensus regarding consistent classification of risks when
applying the taxonomy. Agreement was high (83.3%), with a mean score of 4.32 (SD = 0.52)
and substantial reliability (κ = 0.81, α = 0.82). Level-2 categories were generally
interpreted uniformly across professional backgrounds, though minor variations occurred
between technical analysts (focused on system-compromise behaviors) and compliance
officers (emphasizing regulatory accountability). A few experts proposed simplifying
low-frequency categories (e.g., R5.6 & R5.7) to enhance interpretive consistency.

### (3) Structural Bias and Balance (Q3)
Nine experts (75.0%) agreed that the taxonomy maintains appropriate balance across
cybersecurity, financial crime, and consumer protection dimensions, with a mean score of
4.20 (SD = 0.55) and moderate-to-substantial reliability (κ = 0.73, α = 0.76). The
majority viewed the taxonomy as structurally neutral. A few experts pointed out a relative
overrepresentation of cybersecurity-related subcategories (R1) compared to consumer-facing
harms (R4), recommending a future expansion of consumer-rights-related Level-2 entries to
reflect increasing digital-finance exposure.

### (4) Practical Applicability (Q4)
Eleven of twelve experts (91.7%) rated the taxonomy as highly applicable to real-world
financial safety assessments, with a mean score of 4.59 (SD = 0.44). They highlighted that
the taxonomy's modular JSON-schema design allows seamless integration with AI safety
pipelines and regulatory evaluation systems. Seven experts commended its practicality in
generating structured red-teaming scenarios aligned with FSI's existing supervision
datasets. Overall reliability was quite high (κ = 0.83, α = 0.84), underscoring the
taxonomy's effectiveness for institutional deployment and research reproducibility.
